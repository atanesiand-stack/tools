How to price a pressure washing job

A single price per square foot is easy to quote, but a driveway, a second-story wall and an oil-stained patio do not take the same time per foot. Pricing from time keeps those differences visible. Measure each surface, divide by how many square feet you clean per hour on that kind of surface, and let your costs and margin set the price.

  1. Measure each surface separately: driveway, walkways, patio, siding, deck, fence, roof.
  2. Divide the square feet by your square feet per hour for that surface.
  3. Mark anything slower than normal: heavy staining, second-story work, limited water or access.
  4. Add travel, setup and pack-out time. On a small job it can be the biggest line.
  5. Turn hours into cost: wages, payroll burden, chemicals and fuel, overhead.
  6. Divide the cost by one minus your profit margin, and never go below your minimum job charge.

Which numbers should you use?

The square feet per hour in this calculator are examples so you can see it working. They are not an industry standard. Your machine, your surface cleaner and your water supply decide your real speed. Time two or three jobs, divide the square feet by the hours, and type your own numbers into the rate column.

What the result tells you

  • Break-even cost is what the job costs you with no profit.
  • Job price is the break-even cost plus your margin, rounded to the nearest $5, and never below your minimum.
  • Price per sq ft lets you compare your time-based price with a per-foot price list.
  • Revenue per labor hour shows whether the job is worth loading the trailer.

Questions

Is this calculator free?

Yes. It runs in your browser, there is no sign-up, and the numbers you enter are not sent anywhere.

Should I charge per square foot or per hour?

Customers usually want a fixed price, so quote a fixed price. Use time to work out what that fixed price needs to be.

What is a minimum job charge?

It is the lowest price you accept for showing up. Small jobs still cost you travel and setup time, so the calculator never returns less than your minimum.

Does it include sealing or stain treatment?

No. Those are usually flat-priced extras. The paid Excel workbook has an add-ons table for them.
